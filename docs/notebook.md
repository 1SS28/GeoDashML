## 2026-10-18 to 2026-10-20: Phase 4 RL on the backward curriculum

- Wrote `gdrl/`: PPO in plain PyTorch (clipped surrogate, GAE, one env, advantages normalised per batch), an actor and a critic MLP (256-256 tanh) over the 975-float observation, a `CurriculumDriver` that moves to the earlier checkpoint once 80% of the last 50 episodes from the current one completed, a training loop that logs every stage switch to `train.log` and `stages.jsonl`, metrics read from the env's `episodes.csv`, and `python -m gdrl play`, which runs the policy with rendering on and saves its run as a `gdsearch.Solution` for the A key. No stable-baselines: the stage switches and the attempt bookkeeping sit inside the rollout loop, and the env is a single lockstep client, so a vectorised library buys nothing. The level ID stays out of the observation, so the policy cannot key on the level; a level-ID embedding is a later ablation. `backward_schedule(sol, spacing_ticks=480)` gives 41 checkpoints plus the level start for Stereo Madness, the first at tick 19,806 (96.3%, 133 decisions before the end).
- Throughput check before the first run: the policy's forward pass is 31 µs per decision and a PPO update 70 ms per 2,048 decisions, so the game's round trip is the whole cost, as in Phase 2.
- The gradient is clipped per network (actor and critic separately). With one joint norm the value loss, hundreds of times the policy loss at the start (value loss 928 against a policy loss of 0.001 in the first update of the smoke run), would have set the clip scale and shrunk the actor's step.

A sampled policy has to be nearly perfect at every one of about 25 obstacles for the full suffix to complete 80% of the time, and the entropy bonus kept enough randomness to miss one. With 0.001 the second run passed stage 10 in 455 attempts. The cost showed up one stage earlier: from the checkpoint at 77.9% the first required tap is 22 decisions in, and the less random policy needed 6,448 attempts to find it (5,448 deaths on that one spike), where the first run needed 912. Evaluation episodes are resets like any other, so they count as attempts and have their rows in `episodes.csv`. Seed 0 was resumed from its stage-12 policy with this rule (`--resume`, which continues the episode numbering), as Sanjey asked to continue rather than restart. Its greedy evaluations passed stages 12 and 13 at once. Seed 0 is therefore a hybrid: stages 0 to 11 under the sampled 0.5 rule, 12 onward under the greedy rule, and its optimiser state was reset at the resume. - Search design. The bot keeps a stack of commits, each the longest surviving prefix found so far with its death tick D, and extends a commit by replaying candidate schedules that differ inside a window before D, committing the one that dies latest (`gdsearch/search.py`, README). The first live run exposed the candidate family that matters: with single toggles only (hold or release from decision j onward) the cube's move over the first spike was "hold forever", it bunny-hopped past the spike pair at 975/1005 and died at tick 1108 (x 1436), and no single toggle inside 480 ticks helped, because the fix is a release followed by a well-timed jump: a toggle pair, found after 1770-candidate enumeration started. 290 attempts to reach x 1500. Adding taps (press at j, release at j + 1) as the first family, committed at once when they die at least 16 ticks later than D, gives 65 attempts to the same point and a schedule that reads like a human's: tap, run, tap. Toggles remain the ship's move. Plain depth-first search over decisions was rejected on paper: it enumerates 2^k settings of the k decisions between the needed fix and the death before moving the fix earlier.
- **Surprise 9: the bridge cut off large replies.** The first full search died at 73% with a `JSONDecodeError` 6 MB into a reply and "client detached (send failed)" in the Geode log. A dead cube reproduces it: 20,000-, 50,000- and 100,000-tick traces come back intact (0.4 to 1.2 s), a 150,000-tick trace (6.4 MB) is cut off at about 4.9 MB. Cause: on macOS the accepted socket inherits `O_NONBLOCK` from the listener, so once the socket buffer is full `send` returns `EAGAIN` and `sendAll` treated that as a failure and dropped the client. Bridge 0.1.2 polls for `POLLOUT` and retries; the 150,000-tick trace then arrives in 0.43 s. Phase 1 and 2 never sent more than about 1 MB.
- **Surprise 10: the level never completes in lockstep; the end portal is the real end.** The 6 MB reply was the search doubling a run that stayed alive for 69,000 ticks: the player had reached the end and sat there. Bridge 0.1.2 reports `end_anim` and `locked`; the Python client's `State.finished` is `completed or end_anim`, and GDEnv terminates with the completion bonus on `finished`. `run` also gained `stop: true`, ending a run on the tick that kills the player or reaches the end, which makes every search replay exactly as long as it needs to be and gives the completion tick for free. ## 2026-10-16 (later): Phase 1 bridge

- **Spike result: passed.** Game launched via Steam with Geode 5.11.2 on 2.2081. Geode enabled both hooks for the mod (`GJBaseGameLayer::update`, `CCKeyboardDispatcher::dispatchKeyboardMSG`). In Stereo Madness, P froze the cube with the music still playing; 11 presses of N moved it 1.2982 to 1.2983 units each, which is 311.58 / 240 = 1.29825, the 1x speed per 240 Hz tick. y stayed at 105.0. So one call of `update(1/240)` is exactly one physics tick, with no accumulator drift over 11 steps.
- Launch quirk: opening the .app directly makes the game exit immediately and ask Steam to relaunch it, which Steam did not do. Launch with `open steam://rungameid/322170`. Because the locked manifest still carries 2.209's buildid, Steam believes the game is current and starts it without trying to update.
